# Gameboy Retreat — Marketing Playbook

Reference material for video, email, and brand. Written 2026-09; revisit as things change.

## 1. Video blueprint (repair / restoration content)

Transformation-first. Lead with the finished console — do not build up to it.

```
0:00-0:03   HOOK        Final restored console in hand, already beautiful.
                        Text overlay: "This was $15 and broken"
0:03-0:08   BEFORE      Hard cut to the filthy, dead unit. The contrast IS the content.
0:08-0:35   THE WORK    Fast cuts, satisfying moments only:
                          screws out - shell separating - the dirty-to-clean wipe
                          solder flowing - ribbon cable seating - screen first lighting up
0:35-0:45   THE TEST    Power on, a few seconds of real gameplay.
0:45-0:55   RESULT      Slow rotation of the finished console.
0:55-1:00   CTA         One line, spoken AND on screen.
```

### Rules that matter

- **First three seconds decide everything.** Never open with a slow pan or a talking intro.
- **Silent-legible.** Most people watch muted. Text overlays carry the story.
- **Real sound, not music.** ASMR-adjacent audio massively outperforms music on repair content.
- **One CTA, never two.** Rotate across videos instead of stacking.

### CTA rotation

- "I've got three of these up on my site — link in bio."
- "Send me yours and I'll do this to it."
- "Want to learn this? I do one-on-one sessions."

### SEO specifics

- **Say the model name out loud.** Auto-captions are indexed. "Game Boy Advance SP AGS-101
  backlight mod" is a real search term people type.
- Put the full model name in on-screen text *and* the caption.
- Specific hashtags (`#gbasp #retrohandheld #consolemodding`), never `#fyp`.
- **YouTube Shorts > TikTok for durability.** Shorts are indexed by Google and surface in
  search for years. TikTok views die in a week. Post both; treat YouTube as the asset.
- **Pin a comment with your link.** Most reliable click path on every platform.
- One video per week beats ten posted at once. Consistency is what gets rewarded.

## 2. ASMR audio capture

The sound is doing as much work as the picture. Phone mics will not get you there —
they compress, noise-gate, and auto-level exactly the quiet detail you want.

### Minimum viable setup

| Need | Option | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mic | Lavalier clipped near the work | Cheapest real upgrade. Clip to the bench lip, not yourself. |
| Mic | Small-diaphragm condenser on a desk arm | Better detail; needs an interface. |
| Mic | USB condenser 6-12in from hands | Simplest; watch for desk rumble. |
| Isolation | Shock mount or folded towel under the mic | Bench knocks travel through the desk. |
| Control | Record audio separately from video | Sync in the edit. Frees mic placement entirely. |

### Technique

- **Get close.** 6-12 inches from the hands. Detail falls off fast with distance.
- **Record room tone** — 30 seconds of silence per session. Lets the editor subtract
  fridge hum and HVAC cleanly.
- **Kill the noise floor first.** Fans, AC, PC, phone on silent. Cheaper than any plugin.
- **Slow down deliberately.** Real repair speed is too fast to sound good. Screws turned
  slowly, the shell separated slowly. Perform the action for the mic.
- **Foley the weak ones.** Some sounds are too quiet to capture in situ. Re-record the
  screw-turn or the shell-click in isolation afterwards and lay it in.

### The sounds worth capturing

- Screwdriver bite and turn (tri-wing on Nintendo shells has a distinctive click)
- Shell separating — the plastic creak-and-pop
- Isopropyl on a brush, and the wipe across a dirty board
- Compressed air burst
- Ribbon cable ZIF connector clicking shut
- Solder wick drag; flux sizzle
- Battery cover snap
- Cartridge insertion — the single most nostalgic sound you have
- Power switch click, then the boot chime

### Editing

- Compress lightly to bring detail up; do not brick-wall it.
- High-pass around 80Hz to remove desk rumble.
- Leave the sounds dry-ish — reverb kills intimacy.
- If music is used at all, duck it hard under every foley moment.

## 3. Email list

### Provider

Start with **Resend Broadcasts** — already wired into the app for transactional mail.
Move to MailerLite or Kit only when automations and visual forms are actually needed.
See `docs/` decision notes or ask again when the list passes ~500.

### The offer

Nobody subscribes to a newsletter. They subscribe to get something.

- **"First look at new restorations before they go public."** ← strongest for this business.
  Genuine scarcity: only a handful exist at a time.
- Free guide: *"What to check before buying a used Game Boy"* — captures people who are
  not ready to buy yet.

### Collect in three places

1. Sign-up form on the site (footer + console UI)
2. Link in every social bio
3. Card in every package

### Cadence

Every two weeks. Short is fine:
- what got restored
- what is available now
- one thing learned

A list you never email is worthless — people forget they subscribed and mark it spam.

### Why first-look works here

When a Rayquaza SP comes in, one email to 200 people who asked for exactly that can sell
it before it ever hits the site. No marketplace fees, no algorithm in the way.

## 4. Revenue models

Current and planned, in order of margin:

| Model | Inventory cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lessons (1:1 Zoom) | none | Pure time. Highest margin per hour. |
| Mod/repair service | none | Customer supplies hardware. No capital tied up. |
| Console sales | ~$75/unit | The original business. Capital-limited. |
| Course (future) | none | Build once, sell forever. |

The ceiling on console sales is how many you can source and fund. Services and lessons
have no such ceiling — that is the point of adding them.

## 5. Trust signals

- 30-day warranty on consoles and repair work (live on site)
- Review counts: state only what can be substantiated with screenshots.
  Specific beats vague — "312 sales, 4.9 stars on Mercari" > "hundreds of reviews".
  FTC's 2024 consumer review rule applies to small sellers too.
- Founder-forward: face, hands, name. People buy restored childhood objects from a person.
- Response time commitment (currently 24 hours)
